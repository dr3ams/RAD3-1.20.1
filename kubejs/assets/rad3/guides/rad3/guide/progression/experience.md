# Experience

Experience is the most valuable resource in RAD3. It funds character growth, enchanting, and disenchanting — and your current XP level directly determines your health pool via [Level Hearts](../getting-started/health-bar.md). Dying means losing that progress and the survivability it provided.

## Earning XP

- **Killing mobs** — the primary source, scales with mob difficulty
- **Dungeon chests** — many contain XP Scrolls and similar items
- **Quests** — completing questbook objectives rewards XP directly
- **Quest coins** — coins earned from quests can be exchanged for XP in the Market
- **Vanilla mechanics** — mining ores, smelting, breeding animals, fishing, trading with villagers, and using grindstones all yield XP as normal
- **Eating registered meals** — every registered food item grants a small random XP reward on eat

## Spending XP

- **Passive Skill Tree** — the primary long-term XP sink; nodes cost levels to unlock
- **Enchanting** — standard Minecraft enchanting table and anvil operations
- **Disenchanting and copying** — moving enchantments between items costs XP
- **Quest exchanges** — some questbook trades require XP as payment

## The core risk

Every XP level you hold contributes to your maximum health via Level Hearts. The higher your peak level since your last death, the more bonus hearts you carry. If you die with a large XP pool stored *inside* your level bar rather than in a safe vessel, you lose both the levels and the health they provided.

**Store your XP before risky runs.** This is not optional advice — it is the difference between dying once and dying in a downward spiral where each death leaves you weaker for the next attempt.
